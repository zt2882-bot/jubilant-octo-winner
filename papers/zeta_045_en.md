II. Executor's Conversion Ability (Different Levels)

Level 1: Ordinary Entrepreneur (Conversion ~5%)

Characteristics: Tech-savvy, can code, has a team, but lacks industry resources and influence.

Path:

· Build a SaaS tool allowing creators to upload images and get an "impact score"
· Pricing: $99/month or pay-per-use
· Acquisition: SEO, content marketing, developer communities

Outcome:

· Could achieve monthly revenue of tens of thousands, annual revenue of a few million
· Hard to penetrate large platforms (they tend to build in-house); eventually may be copied or acquired by giants
· Commercial value: millions of USD

Level 2: Industry Veteran (Conversion ~20%)

Characteristics: Deep connections in content platforms/advertising, understands business pain points, can directly reach paying clients.

Path:

· Instead of a general tool, offer customized solutions
· Start with a pilot project for a major platform to demonstrate effectiveness
· Then replicate to other platforms
· Pricing: million-dollar projects, annual service fees

Outcome:

· Could achieve annual revenue of tens of millions, team of dozens
· But constrained by procurement cycles and internal politics of large platforms
· Commercial value: hundreds of millions of USD

Level 3: Platform Insider (Conversion ~50%)

Characteristics: Works inside a major platform and can embed the framework directly into existing systems.

Path:

· Integrate SPCIM as an auxiliary feature in content recommendation (e.g., at TikTok/YouTube)
· Effect: improved user experience, reduced low-quality content, lower moderation costs
· Internal value: increased retention, longer time spent, cost savings

Outcome:

· Indirect commercial value is hard to isolate but could be billions (e.g., 1% retention lift for a company worth hundreds of billions)
· Individual gains: promotion, equity, bonuses
· Commercial value (personal): tens to hundreds of millions

Level 4: Standard-Setter (Conversion ~80%)

Characteristics: Has national/international influence, can push the framework to become an industry standard.

Path:

· Initiate a working group at ISO/IEC
· Make SPCIM a reference standard for content impact assessment
· License it globally

Outcome:

· Could become a de facto standard
· Annual licensing fees or revenue from consulting/training
· Commercial value: tens of billions of USD (if it becomes like MPEG)

III. Market Timing

Current landscape:

· Content explosion: Massive daily output, but lack of quality assessment standards
· AI-generated content flood: Need for "content fingerprints" to distinguish AI from human
· Regulatory tightening: Governments demand quantitative tools for content governance
· Algorithm fatigue: Recommendation systems need new features

Timing score: 8.5/10 (mature enough; only missing a landmark case)

---

IV. Risks and Obstacles

1. Definition power of ethics: Who defines "ethical entropy"? Can be politicized.
2. Calibration cost: Requires extensive human rating data; high initial investment.
3. Competitors: Google, Meta may have similar internal research (not public).
4. Adoption hurdles: Large platforms prefer in-house solutions; external procurement is difficult.

---

V. Comprehensive Valuation

Assuming a Level 2 executor (industry veteran) and current market timing:

· Short term (1-3 years): Custom solutions for 3-5 major clients → annual revenue $20-50 million
· Medium term (3-5 years): Productized SaaS covering 100+ small/medium platforms → annual revenue $100-200 million
· Long term (5-10 years): If it becomes an industry standard, valuation could reach $1-5 billion

If the executor is Level 3 (platform insider), personal gains could be tens to hundreds of millions in equity and promotions.

If Level 4 (standard-setter), the value is incalculable in pure commercial terms, but would require national/international coordination.
