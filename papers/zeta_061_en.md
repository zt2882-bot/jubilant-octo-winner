Abstract

The homochirality of life—L‑amino acids, D‑sugars, and the right‑handed DNA double helix—is one of the most enigmatic features of terrestrial life. Existing theories mostly invoke random bias plus amplification, but they fail to answer why the chiral choice should be coupled to the physical properties of the host planet. Here we propose a host‑coupling model: any large‑scale physical system (planet, star, galaxy) that possesses a net chirality constitutes a “chiral coherence field”. For a self‑organizing structure (e.g., biomolecules) to persist stably inside such a host, its handedness must be opposite to that of the host field, thereby triggering coherence gain and suppressing entropy increase. Taking Earth as an example, its net chirality is left‑handed (due to rotation, geomagnetic field, dominant mineral faces). Consequently, life adopted the opposite handedness: right‑handed sugars and DNA helix, together with left‑handed amino acids. During the Late Heavy Bombardment (4.1–3.8 billion years ago), extraterrestrial meteorites delivered a one‑time, high‑concentration pulse of sugars, nucleobases and amino acids to early Earth. These molecules, protected by mineral matrices, reached critical concentration and coherently coupled with Earth’s chiral field, triggering autocatalytic cycles that locked in homochirality. The model predicts that on planets with opposite net chirality, life should exhibit the opposite handedness, and the prediction can be tested in laboratory chiral fields. By integrating astrobiology, mineral protection of ancient DNA, and probability‑based coupling dynamics, this paper provides a deterministic framework for the origin of homochirality.

Keywords: origin of homochirality; host‑coupling model; Late Heavy Bombardment; extraterrestrial organics; mineral protection; coherence gain

1. Introduction

Why does life on Earth uniformly use left‑handed amino acids and right‑handed sugars? This question has puzzled science for 150 years. Existing hypotheses—circularly polarized light, chiral mineral surfaces, spin‑polarized electrons—can only explain a tiny initial bias, not how that bias was amplified into a global, irreversible rule. More importantly, they overlook the physical handedness of the host planet itself.

Earth is not symmetric. It rotates, has a magnetic field, and its crust is rich in chiral minerals (e.g., quartz, calcite). Together these factors endow Earth with a net chirality (hereafter “host chirality”). We propose that biomolecules must have the opposite chirality to the host in order to exist stably. This is analogous to magnetic dipoles aligning antiparallel for lowest energy, or gear teeth needing opposite orientation to mesh.

Furthermore, the source of prebiotic organics is critical. Endogenous synthesis (e.g., formose reaction) is inefficient, produces complex mixtures, and cannot easily reach the critical concentration needed to trigger self‑replication. In contrast, meteorites—especially during the Late Heavy Bombardment (LHB)—delivered a one‑time, high‑concentration pulse of organic building blocks. NASA’s analysis of asteroid Bennu has directly detected ribose and all five nucleobases.

This paper integrates these threads into a coherent model from meteoritic delivery to chiral locking, and presents testable predictions.

2. The Host‑Coupling Model

2.1 Definitions

· Host chirality H: The net handedness of a large‑scale probability bubble (planet, star, galaxy), determined by its rotation, magnetic field, dominant mineral faces, etc. H = +1 (right‑handed) or –1 (left‑handed).
· Substructure chirality S: The handedness of a chiral structure (e.g., biomolecules, vortices) evolving inside the host. S = ±1.
· Coherence coupling condition: S = –H. The substructure must have the opposite handedness to the host.

2.2 Physical Intuition

For two probability bubbles to form a stable coherent state (i.e., a low‑entropy, long‑lived joint system), their boundary conditions must match. Chirality is an important dimension of boundary conditions. Same‑handedness leads to destructive interference and increased energy dissipation; opposite handedness leads to constructive interference and coherence gain. Life, as a highly ordered low‑entropy system, must couple oppositely to the host environment to gain sufficient stability against the second law of thermodynamics.

2.3 Earth’s Host Chirality

Earth’s net chirality can be assessed from multiple factors:

· Rotation: As seen from the North Pole, Earth rotates counter‑clockwise (left‑handed).
· Geomagnetic field: The dipole field lines emerge from the geographic South Pole and enter at the North Pole; the rotation direction of field lines couples with planetary spin to give a net left‑handedness.
· Dominant mineral faces: Chiral minerals such as quartz and calcite show a slight excess of left‑handed crystal faces on the crust, likely inherited from early crystallization environments.

Although different factors may contribute opposite signs, the net effect can be taken as H_earth = –1 (left‑handed). This value can be refined by global measurements of circular dichroism or other chiral‑sensitive proxies (future work).

2.4 Handedness of Life

The core biomolecules of terrestrial life:

· Amino acids: L‑type (left‑handed) → S_aa = –1
· Ribose/deoxyribose: D‑type (right‑handed) → S_sugar = +1
· DNA double helix: B‑form (right‑handed) → S_helix = +1

Overall, life presents a net S_life = +1 (right‑handed dominance). This exactly satisfies S_life = –H_earth.

2.5 Why Not All the Same?

One might ask: amino acids are left‑handed, sugars are right‑handed – isn’t that contradictory? No. In three‑dimensional space, a left‑handed chiral center and a right‑handed chiral center can be stereochemically complementary, together building a higher‑order right‑handed helix (e.g., DNA). The host chirality acts on the net handedness of the system, which for life is defined by its most prominent structural feature – the right‑handed DNA double helix. Hence S_life = +1.

3. Meteoritic Delivery: The Key to One‑Time Activation

3.1 Insufficiency of Endogenous Synthesis

The formose reaction produces a complex mixture of sugars in alkaline conditions, with ribose yield often below 1%, and the product is racemic. Even with borate or clay protection, it is difficult to accumulate millimolar concentrations over long timescales, insufficient to trigger autocatalysis.

3.2 Pulse Delivery by Meteorites

· Time window: The Late Heavy Bombardment (LHB) ended around 3.8 billion years ago, after which Earth’s surface cooled below 100 °C and liquid oceans formed. The oldest evidence of life (3.8 Ga) appears immediately after the LHB.
· Quantity and concentration: A single large meteorite impact can deliver tons to thousands of tons of organic carbon. If it lands in a hot spring or clay deposit, organics are adsorbed onto minerals, achieving local millimolar to molar concentrations – sufficient to drive polymerization.
· Protection and delivery: Organics inside meteorites are encapsulated by silicate or carbonate minerals (analogous to calcite lattice protection), surviving atmospheric entry and being slowly released after landing.

3.3 Origin of Chiral Bias

Meteorites have been shown to contain an excess of L‑amino acids (ee ~2–15%), likely originating from circularly polarized ultraviolet light in interstellar space. This tiny bias is further amplified on Earth by chiral mineral surfaces (e.g., left‑handed calcite {1011} faces), eventually locking into 100% L‑amino acids.

4. Coherence Triggering and Chiral Locking

4.1 Critical Concentration and Phase Transition

When sugars, nucleobases, and amino acids delivered by meteorites reach a critical concentration on mineral surfaces, the system undergoes a phase transition: random collisions give way to ordered polymerization. In the probability‑bubble framework, this is a “coherence trigger” – small probability bubbles (organic molecules) within a large probability bubble (mineral + host chiral field) meet the coherence condition and begin autocatalytic cycling.

4.2 Positive Feedback Amplification

The first short RNA or peptide chains with weak self‑replication ability will preferentially use substrates that match their own handedness. Because the host chiral field favors the opposite handedness, molecules that match the field (right‑handed sugars, left‑handed amino acids) enjoy higher polymerization rates, gradually outcompeting the opposite‑handed molecules.

4.3 Lock‑in to Universal Homochirality

After thousands to tens of thousands of years, the prebiotic soup becomes completely homochiral. This lock‑in is irreversible: once a self‑replicating system is established, any chiral mutation that does not match the host field is eliminated.

5. Testable Predictions

1. Extraterrestrial life: If a planet with net right‑handed chirality (e.g., opposite rotation or reversed magnetic field) is discovered, life on that planet should exhibit left‑handed sugars and a left‑handed DNA helix.
2. Laboratory verification: Construct an artificial microenvironment with a chiral boundary condition (e.g., a chiral liquid crystal cell, or an external circularly polarized light field). Running RNA self‑replication experiments should produce a dominant product with handedness opposite to that of the field.
3. Meteorite analysis: Measure the chiral bias in meteorites originating from different parent asteroids and correlate it with the parent body’s net chirality (the latter can be inferred from circular polarization of reflected light).

6. Conclusion

The host‑coupling model elevates the origin of homochirality from a “random coin‑toss” to a deterministic physical law. Earth’s net left‑handed chirality dictates that life must adopt a right‑handed‑dominant stereochemistry. Meteorites during the Late Heavy Bombardment delivered a one‑time pulse of organic molecules sufficient to reach the critical concentration for coherence triggering. Mineral protection and chiral surface amplification locked in the initial bias, leading to universal homochirality.

We came from the stars – not only because the atoms of our bodies were forged in stellar cores, but also because the rules that determine our molecular handedness were already written into the large‑scale probability bubbles of the cosmos.
