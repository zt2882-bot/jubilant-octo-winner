## 1 Introduction: Gravity Is Not a Force

Traditional physics treats gravity as one of the four fundamental interactions, described by a universal constant G. The probabilistic-dynamics framework offers a different picture: **gravity is not a "force" but the macroscopic manifestation of a probabilistic-intensity gradient.**

An underlying constraint-topological structure exists. This structure produces a distribution of probabilistic intensity; the gradient (orientation) of this intensity manifests macroscopically as gravity. The "shape" and "range" of gravity are therefore determined by the topological structure of the object (its size and mass distribution), rather than by an externally imposed constant G acting uniformly.

Einstein said "matter tells spacetime how to curve." We say it more thoroughly: **structure tells probability how to orient, and that orientation is gravity.**

## 2 The Lunar Case: The Arithmetic Necessity of 1/6 Gravity

The Moon's surface gravity is about 1/6 that of Earth. This is not a coincidence; it is the arithmetic necessity of "structure determines orientation."

The Moon's mass is about 1/81 of Earth's, and its radius about 1/3.7 of Earth's. Surface gravity satisfies:

$$g = \frac{GM}{R^2}$$

Substituting the Moon-to-Earth ratios:

$$\frac{g_{moon}}{g_{earth}} = \frac{M_{moon}/M_{earth}}{(R_{moon}/R_{earth})^2} = \frac{1/81}{(1/3.7)^2} \approx \frac{1}{6}$$

The key point: **M and R are both structural parameters of the object** — mass is the quantity of matter, radius is the size. The surface-gravity formula is therefore essentially "surface gravity = a function of the object's topological structure," not "the formula of a universal force."

Small Moon → narrow probabilistic-bubble boundary → small probabilistic-intensity gradient at the boundary → 1/6 gravity.
Large Earth → wide boundary → large gradient → 1 gravity.

The shape and range of gravity are determined by the object's own topological structure. This is "the materialization of topological-structural probabilistic orientation."

## 3 The So-Called "Anomaly": The Lunar Density Puzzle

The Moon's mean density is 3.34 g/cm³ versus Earth's 5.52 g/cm³ — a difference of nearly half. This density gap has long been treated as "anomalous," even spawning the "hollow Moon" fringe hypothesis.

Key data:

| Metric | Earth | Moon |
|---|---|---|
| Mean density (g/cm³) | 5.52 | 3.34 |
| Iron-core mass fraction | 32% | <2% |
| Moment of inertia I/MR² | 0.33 | 0.393 |

A moment of inertia of 0.393 is very close to that of a uniform sphere (0.4), indicating that the Moon's **interior density is relatively uniform** — it lacks the dense iron core Earth has.

The standard explanation is the giant-impact hypothesis: the Moon condensed from **mantle material** blasted off the early Earth, hence its lack of an iron core and its low density.

**Not hollow — just coreless.**

## 4 The Hollow-Moon Hypothesis: Right Direction, Wrong Conclusion

The "hollow Moon" hypothesis gets its intuition **right** — the Moon's interior mass distribution does differ from a "uniform expectation with a dense core."

But it pushes the conclusion to the wrong side: not "cavity," but "absence of a dense core."

Within the "gravity = topological-structural probabilistic orientation" framework, the issue can be stated more precisely:

> Gravity fields superpose. An object's external gravity topology depends on the **spatial distribution** of its interior mass, not its mean density.

The Moon's gravity-topological structure may therefore exhibit "cancellation" or "amplification" effects due to its interior mass distribution — but here "cancellation" does not mean "a cavity causing gravity to vanish," but rather "the absence of a dense core causing the gravity topology to deviate from uniform expectation."

## 5 Methodology: Anomaly = Reference-Model Error

The core methodological proposition of this paper:

> **A so-called "anomaly" is usually a wrong reference model. Measured against a "uniform expectation" or an "Earth analogy," any self-consistent structure appears anomalous; switch to a "structure-consistent" framework, and the anomaly dissolves.**

- Against "Earth" as reference → the Moon lacks an iron core → looks "anomalous / hollow"

This methodology extends beyond the Moon. It applies to all "celestial anomalies": dark matter, galactic rotation curves, the Pioneer anomaly… the root of many "puzzles" is that we mistook the predictions of a simplified model for "nature's baseline."
